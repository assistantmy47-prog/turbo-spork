# My working context

Add your role, workstreams, stakeholders, synonyms, escalation paths and deadlines. For example, a governance lead may track records retention and data quality as separate workstreams.

## Priority

- High: a regulatory or external deadline, a material incident, or a decision I own that blocks delivery. Name the evidence.
- Normal: a concrete commitment I own or a material change requiring my attention.
- Low: routine scheduling, updates without a material change, and background information.
- Flag time-sensitive prep for today's meetings. RSVP only when needed. Exclude cancelled or declined events from today's agenda.

## Actions and status

- provide_information: I owe a reply, factual update, or document.
- approve_reject: I am the decision maker or approver.
- execute: I committed to delivering something.
- investigate: I need to establish facts or diagnose an issue.
- no_action: use information_only, acknowledgement_only, historical_completed, or no_obligation.
- Ownership must be supported by an explicit assignment or commitment. Being copied, invited, or mentioned does not establish ownership.
- Status is open or closed. Record what is blocking an open action. An acknowledgement or promise to act does not close it. Close only with evidence of delivery, withdrawal, reassignment or supersession.
- Reuse the open commitment key when a later record updates the same obligation. Leave uncertain inferred links unresolved.
- Treat comparable obligations in mail and Planner as one when the evidence supports it. Prefer the newest explicit evidence. Avoid marking a closed obligation open based on an older record.
- Draft concise professional replies. Use placeholders for missing facts. For important information with no action, record what changed and why it matters.
